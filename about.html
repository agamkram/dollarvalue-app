DollarValue

Item is the thing. In terms of is the measure. The lists match, except In terms of starts with CPI, PCE, chained CPI, and the GDP deflator.

The answer is the level then, scaled by the measure. A year with no print shows a dash and says when the series starts or ends. A month with no print is a dash. October 2025 CPI was never published.

Year means the average of that year’s prints. An open year is marked YTD. A year missing months is marked partial. On NASDAQ, the Dow, and Bitcoin the month is the last print and the year is the average of every print. From September 2016 the S&P month is the average of daily closes.

Gold is the US official price through 1973, then the World Bank monthly average. That column was the London afternoon fixing and is now a spot average of daily rates. Silver is the London afternoon fixing. Bitcoin is Bitstamp from 2011 through November 2014, then Coinbase. The S&P is Shiller’s monthly composite until September 2016, then the S&P 500. The Dow is a consistent daily close from February 1885, and FRED’s ten-year window after that. Neither index includes dividends.

M1 is blank across the May 2020 redefinition, as the item or as the measure.

GDP, M1, M2, and the monetary base are billions of dollars. GDP per capita, household income, and household spending are current dollars. Household income is the Census nominal median from 1967, so earlier years will not match Census’s inflation-adjusted chart. Wheat is US hard red winter and corn is maize, World Bank prices from 1960, then the IMF series. Household spending is the average annual outlay of a consumer unit, 1984 through 2024. The median home price is quarterly, along with the deflator, GDP per capita, and FHFA. January, April, July, and October on those series are Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4. GDP and household spending are annual. PCE, the deflator, and GDP per capita are seasonally adjusted. CPI on the screen is CPI-U, all urban consumers, and it is not seasonally adjusted. Chained CPI is revised after it is published.

The hourly wage is the private production-worker wage from 1964. Before that it is the hourly pay of factory production workers, including benefits, scaled so 1964 meets the private wage. Work time uses that wage, and before 1964 the line says factory pay. A month the measure does not print is gray. A year with no months snaps to Year. Ounces and coins are shown only for a dollar price. Ahead and Behind are the three things furthest from the measure. Per year when the span is a year or longer. Over a shorter span the figure is the total change. Under that, one person's share of the federal debt is the debt held by the public divided by the population. The middle figure is that share in the later year's dollars, or that month's dollars when a month is chosen. When the measure is not CPI, the label says CPI. The wage row is that wage in the same terms. Debt and tax are also shown in hours of the wage for those years. Taxes per person are federal receipts divided by the population. Work time on a custom dollar is the minutes to earn that dollar.

US only. CPI from 1800 is the Minneapolis Fed scaled onto BLS from 1913. Data: BLS, BEA, FRED, FHFA, Census, IMF, World Bank, Minneapolis Fed, Shiller, MeasuringWorth, Officer, OMB, Bitstamp. Gasoline is the BLS average price.
