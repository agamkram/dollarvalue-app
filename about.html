DollarValue

Item is the thing. In terms of is the measure. The lists match, except In terms of starts with CPI-U, PCE, chained CPI, and the GDP deflator.

The answer is the level then, scaled by the measure. A year with no print shows a dash and says when the series starts or ends. A month with no print is a dash. October 2025 CPI was never published.

Year means the average of that year’s prints. An open year is marked YTD. A year missing months is marked partial. On a daily series the month is the last print, and the year is the average of every print.

Gold is the US official price through 1973, then the World Bank monthly average. That column was the London afternoon fixing and is now a spot average of daily rates. Silver is the London afternoon fixing. Bitcoin is Bitstamp from 2011 through November 2014, then Coinbase. The S&P is Shiller’s monthly composite until September 2016, then the S&P 500. The Dow is only the ten years FRED may carry. Neither index includes dividends.

The broad dollar is the Fed’s goods-only index before 2006, scaled onto the goods-and-services index. It rises when the dollar strengthens. M1 is blank across the May 2020 redefinition, as the item or as the measure.

GDP, M1, M2, and the monetary base are billions of dollars. GDP per capita, household income, and household spending are current dollars. Household income is nominal, so earlier years will not match Census’s inflation-adjusted chart. Household spending is the average annual outlay of a consumer unit, 1984 through 2024. The median home price is quarterly, along with the deflator, GDP per capita, and FHFA. January, April, July, and October on those series are Q1, Q2, Q3, and Q4. GDP and household spending are annual. PCE, the deflator, and GDP per capita are seasonally adjusted. CPI-U is not. Chained CPI is revised after it is published.

Work time uses the production-worker wage, which starts in 1964. Ounces and coins are shown only for a dollar price. Pills are the pace versus the measure: per year when the span is a year or longer, colored past 2% a year. Over a shorter span they are the total change, colored past 8%.

US only. CPI from 1800 is the Minneapolis Fed scaled onto BLS from 1913. Data: BLS, BEA, FRED, FHFA, Census, IMF, World Bank, Minneapolis Fed, Shiller, Bitstamp. Gasoline is the BLS average price.
