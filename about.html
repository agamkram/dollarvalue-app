DollarValue

The BLS inflation calculator answers one question with one series. This app keeps the dates and the amount, and lets you choose what the amount is measured in.

Item is the thing you are asking about. In terms of is how you measure it. The two lists match, except In terms of starts with the official price measures — CPI-U, PCE, chained CPI, and the GDP deflator — because those define the dollar and are not themselves a sticker price. Under them sit the same real series as Item: groceries, oil, wheat, corn, gold, silver, bitcoin, wages, income, GDP, money, homes, and the stock indexes.

One main answer. The thing’s dollars then, scaled by whatever you picked In terms of. CPI answers “did this beat official inflation?” Gold answers “did this beat gold’s dollar price?” Same math; one measure at a time. Gold, silver, and Bitcoin also show how many ounces or coins that dollar amount bought then and now. The colored pills show other items under that same measure — what ran hot or cold versus what the measure said should have happened.

Sticker prices also show expected versus actual, and how many minutes of production-worker pay that price took. Index levels (rent, Case-Shiller, college, medical, used cars, NASDAQ, S&P, Dow) and money aggregates are not sticker dollars, so they have no work-time line. Median home sales price and household income are dollars.

US only. CPI from 1800 is Minneapolis Fed scaled onto BLS from 1913. Gold is the official US parity until 1973, then COMEX (a gap in the 1974–1999 market series is left empty, not invented). A month with no print is a dash. GDP is annual. The deflator, GDP per capita, and FHFA home prices are quarterly. The latest year is marked YTD while its last month is still missing. M1 across May 2020 is left blank; the Fed changed the definition that month. Short series (bitcoin, silver, the S&P, the Dow, the broad dollar) only fill years they actually cover. Missing years show as a dash.

Data: BLS, BEA, FRED, FHFA, Census, IMF, Minneapolis Fed. Gasoline on the wheel is the BLS average price. Not ShadowStats, not Chapwood.
