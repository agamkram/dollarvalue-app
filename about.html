DollarValue

The BLS inflation calculator answers one question with one series. This app keeps that question — an amount in one year versus another — and lets you swap the yardstick.

CPI-U is the default because that is the public language. PCE, chained CPI, the GDP deflator, wages, GDP per capita, GDP share, gold, and the monetary aggregates are sitting on the same wheel. They are not the same question. A price index scales a grocery basket. Gold, silver, and Bitcoin also count the amount in ounces or BTC, then and now. M2 asks how much currency was created.

Sticker prices (milk, gas, and the rest) also show expected versus actual, and how many minutes of production-worker pay that price took. Rent, home prices, college, medical care, used cars, and the stock indexes are index levels, not dollars, so they have no work-time line.

US only. CPI from 1800 is Minneapolis Fed scaled onto BLS from 1913. Gold is the official US parity until 1973, then COMEX (a gap in the 1974–1999 market series is left empty, not invented). A month with no print is a dash. Share of GDP is annual. The deflator, GDP per capita, and FHFA home prices are quarterly. The latest year is marked YTD while its last month is still missing. M1 across May 2020 is left blank; the Fed changed the definition that month. Missing years show as a dash.

Data: BLS, BEA, FRED, FHFA, Minneapolis Fed. Gasoline on the wheel is the BLS average price. Not ShadowStats, not Chapwood.
